"He transformed a coastline of dunes into one of South America's most exclusive neighborhoods."

Alfredo Arocena was a lawyer, entrepreneur, banker and politician. His most enduring achievement was neither legal nor financial — it was urban. He saw what Carrasco could become long before anyone else, and he built it anyway.

The name of this firm is not incidental. It is intentional. We work the way he worked: with patience, with a long horizon, and absolute conviction in what we are building.

1869
The man behind
the neighborhood.

Alfredo Arocena is born in Uruguay. A lawyer by training, his greatest legacy will be neither legal nor financial — it will be urban. He will spend his life building institutions, and one neighborhood, that outlast him by a century.

1905
An institution built
for the long term.

He founds the Banco de la Caja Obrera. He will lead it for nearly three decades — a demonstration of his ability to sustain projects across time. The same patience he will later bring to Carrasco.

Years at the helm28 years
1907
An undeveloped
coastline.

He purchases a vast expanse of land on Montevideo's eastern coast. Dunes, marshland, open shore. Where others see nothing, Arocena sees the future. His model: Ostende, Belgium.

ReferenceOstende, Belgium
1912
Carlos Thays
and the garden city.

He founds the Balneario Carrasco Company with Esteban Elena and José Ordeig. He commissions French landscape architect Carlos Thays to design the neighborhood: curved avenues, green space, urban logic unprecedented in Uruguay. Construction of the Hotel begins the same year.

Urban designerCarlos Thays · Paris
1921
The most ambitious
hotel in South America.

The First World War interrupts construction between 1914 and 1918. Arocena does not abandon the project. In 1921, the Hotel Casino Carrasco opens. Today it operates as the Sofitel Montevideo Casino Carrasco & Spa.

TodaySofitel Montevideo
Legacy
From dunes
to Carrasco.

Arocena transformed a shapeless coast into Uruguay's most exclusive residential neighborhood. His urban planning continues to define Carrasco more than a century later. The neighborhood bears the name of its colonial landowner — but its soul has a different name.

The avenue that carries his nameAvenida Arocena

Why we do what we do

For generations, our family has understood land and architecture not merely as assets, but as places that gather families, create identity and build a shared story. Fields and homes have been meeting places — and a source of stability across generations.

The true family legacy does not reside in the properties themselves, but in the values our ancestors sought to pass down. Today, we bring that same long-term perspective to every search, evaluation and negotiation we undertake on your behalf.
